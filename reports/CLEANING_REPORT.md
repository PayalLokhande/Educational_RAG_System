### 3\. The Iterative Data Audit & Cleansing Pipeline

### A. The Automated Preprocessing Script

Our group built and executed a specialized Python Pandas cleaning engine (`src/data_cleaning.ipynb`) to process the incoming collection sets independently from one another. Running this notebook completely automated our text-scrubbing parameters:

*   **Trailing Whitespace Elimination:** Deployed `.str.strip()` to sanitize all text inputs and eliminate trailing spacing anomalies.
*   **Null Value Extraction:** Executed `.dropna(subset=["Question"])` to purge any row configurations missing primary string inputs.
*   **De-duplication Engine:** Utilized `.drop_duplicates(subset=["Question"])` to guarantee absolute question uniqueness and eliminate redundancy.

### B. Group Quality Case Study 1: Resolving the Viva Rubric Duplication

During our initial mid-sprint data check, our group's manual quality review captured a defect in the Project Viva raw data: the conversion tool utilized had duplicated an identical generic text block under the `Model Answer` and `Keywords` fields for over 70% of the rows.

*   **Resolution Path:** Our group paused the code deployment line and adjusted our data mapping strategy. Because project viva questions are completely dependent on a user's custom report, our group decided to frame these columns as **Universal Prompt Evaluation Rubrics** matched to specific question themes rather than literal student answers. Manasvi Korde updated the Excel sheet with these distinct, customized evaluation criteria (e.g., mapping specific parameters like `bottleneck, constraint, limit` strictly for limitation checks), and Siddhi Parab re-executed the Pandas block to instantly update the storage tier with high-utility grading data.

### C. Group Quality Case Study 2: Mitigating the Technical Domain Imbalance

Upon running Payal Lokhande's data insights script, our data diversity charts uncovered two major layout flaws:

1.  **Extreme Fragmentation:** The technical topic list was originally fractured into 68 unique sub-topic strings, which would break our upcoming web-portal filtering endpoints due to extreme fragmentation.
2.  **Severe Subject Skew:** Data Structures & Algorithms (DSA) single-handedly occupied over 41% of the entire dataset (31 questions), while core pillars like DBMS (8 questions) and OOPs (9 questions) were severely under-represented and starved for data volume.

*   **Resolution Path:** Our group coded a custom regex-driven string tracking function (`standardize_topics`) directly into Siddhi Parab's pipeline to collapse all 68 fragmented entries into our 5 core engineering pillars. To permanently fix the subject skew, Siddhi Shelar executed a targeted data expansion to inject exactly 7 new DBMS rows and 6 new OOPs rows into the collection layer. This data expansion was fed back into Siddhi Parab's automated script, completely neutralizing the topic scarcity risk, stabilizing the database volume, and ensuring beautifully balanced data weights across the whole track.
